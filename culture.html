How people wear shawls around the world

Styles vary by region, family and occasion. These are common traditions, not strict rules.

Pakistan

Women: shawl or chadar over a shalwar kameez, often for weddings. Men: wool or khaddar shawl over both shoulders at Nikkah and winter events. In Sindh, the ajrak is worn by both.

Kashmir

Known for pashmina and fine needlework. Both women and men wrap shawls over the traditional pheran in winter.

India

Women: shawls over sarees and kurtis. In Himachal, Kullu shawls are worn wrapped and pinned. Men: plain or patterned shawls over a kurta.

Bangladesh

Traditionally worn by both men and women. Shawls with all-over patterns are draped over the shoulders or wrapped around the body.

Andes (Peru, Bolivia)

Women: woven manta shawls, fastened at the chest. Men: ponchos in place of shawls.

Mexico

Women: the rebozo, a long woven shawl used as a wrap, a cover and for carrying babies.

Scotland and Ireland

Tartan plaids and wool shawls. Historically men wore a plaid over the shoulder and women wore shawls over their dress.

Spain

Women: the embroidered, fringed mantón, worn over the shoulders for flamenco and festivals.

East Africa

Maasai men and women wear the shuka, a checked or striped blanket wrap, over the shoulders or around the body.

Arab Gulf

Men: a bisht cloak over a thobe for formal events. Women: a shayla scarf or shawl with an abaya.
